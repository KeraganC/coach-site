Needs versus strategies

A need can be honored in more than one way.

Need

What matters underneath

Closeness · respect · reliability · autonomy · care

→
Strategy

Multiple ways to meet it

A request · a boundary · a conversation · a pause · stepping back

A "no" to your specific request can reflect a "yes" to the other person's need.

Growth versus destiny beliefs

Destiny

A relationship is either a fit or it isn’t.

Conflict can feel like proof that something is fundamentally wrong.

Hard conversations may seem pointless or threatening.

Growth

Relationships can deepen through effort, learning, honesty, and repair.

Conflict can reveal what needs attention.

Hard conversations may become part of how intimacy grows.

Most of us can hold both beliefs at different times.

Action

How do I approach the conversation from a grounded, curious, and compassionate place?

Nonviolent Communication

Express honestly. Listen empathically.

Focus on observations, feelings, needs, and requests—rather than blame or judgment.

“A way of communicating that leads us to give from the heart.”
Marshall Rosenberg

It helps us distinguish between…

What happenedandthe story we tell about it

What we feelandwhat we believe about them

What deeply mattersandthe solution we are attached to

A genuine requestandan attempt to control the outcome

Observation → Feeling → Need → Request

Example

Instead of

“You never ask about me or my life. You don’t care about me, or this relationship.”

You might say

“The last few times we’ve talked, you haven’t asked me questions about my life. I feel sad and disappointed. Mutual interest and curiosity matter to me. Would you be open to showing more curiosity about me and my life?”

Honest

Clear

Open

Can I hear their answer and stay connected to myself?

Discernment

Not every hurt requires a conversation.

Purpose

Safety

Is this relationship safe enough for honest expression?

Choice

Would speaking, writing, waiting, setting a boundary, or stepping back serve me best?

Safety matters. You do not owe direct confrontation to someone who is abusive, coercive, retaliatory, or unsafe.
